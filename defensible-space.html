The three zones, in plain English

California asks homeowners in wildfire areas to keep a cleared buffer around the house, measured out to 100 feet where the property allows. It is split into three zones, and the closer the zone, the stricter it gets.

The point is not a tidy yard. It is giving a fire nothing to climb on as it approaches the house, and giving firefighters somewhere safe to stand if they defend it.

0

The first 5 feet

The ember zone. Most homes are lost to embers landing here, not to a wall of flame.

1
ZONE

5 to 30 feet

The lean, clean and green zone. Plants are fine here — continuous fuel is not.

2
ZONE

30 to 100 feet

The reduced fuel zone. Thinned rather than cleared — the goal is to slow a fire down.

This checklist is a plain-English guide to help you see where your property stands. It is not a legal inspection. For the official requirements, and for anything that affects insurance, check with CAL FIRE, Tuolumne County Fire and your own insurer.

    Why this matters here

    We live in a forest, and the forest keeps growing

    Pine, cedar and oak drop something every month of the year. A property that was clear three summers ago is rarely clear now — needle litter builds up quietly until it is several inches deep.

    • Most homes lost in wildfire are ignited by embers landing on or near the house, not by the fire front itself.
    • Embers travel well ahead of a fire, which is why the first five feet matter more than the last fifty.
    • Clearing is not a one-off. It is seasonal work, like gutters — do it once and you still have to do it again.
