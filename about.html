Our story

A true jack of all trades

For Bryan Wainwright, working with his hands has never just been a job. For nearly a decade, he has been learning, building, fixing, painting, maintaining, and figuring things out along the way. With 8–10 years of hands-on experience gained through both professional work and teaching himself new skills, Bryan has become what many would call a true jack of all trades.

He especially loves painting and hands-on work—the kind of work where he can see a project go from an idea or a problem to something finished and done right.

Bryan does the work himself. There is no call center and no rotating crew — you call the number on this page and he answers it.

Why the business came to be

Building something around the family

For a while, Bryan was doing handyman work on the side while searching for a full-time job that could provide enough income for him to support his family. But as a husband and father of two, with another baby on the way, finding a traditional job that offered both the income and flexibility his family needed became increasingly difficult.

Then came a different idea.

While Bryan was trying to figure out what came next, his wife had a vision of the two of them building a business together instead of Bryan continuing to work for someone else. The more they talked about it, the more it made sense. Bryan already had the skills, the experience, and the love for the work. What he needed was an opportunity to build something sustainable around his family—not work his family around someone else’s schedule.

And so, Wainwright’s Handyman & Land Management was born.

The name is simple: Wainwright is our family name, and this business is something we are building as a family.
What we stand for

Honest work. Clear communication. Do it right.

Our goal is to provide honest, dependable work for the people in our community while giving Bryan the opportunity to do what he loves. Whether it’s a home repair, a painting project, a handyman job, or managing and maintaining the land around a property, Bryan approaches each project with the same principles.

On the job

What that looks like in practice

He shows up

If Bryan says Tuesday morning, it is Tuesday morning. If something goes wrong, you get a call rather than silence.

Price before work

A written figure first, every time. You always know what you are agreeing to before anything begins.

Plain answers

No jargon and no pressure. Ask as many questions as you like — that is part of the job, not an interruption.

Left tidy

Debris loaded and hauled off, tools packed up, and a walk-through together before he leaves.

Working with older neighbors

A lot of this work is for people who used to do it themselves

Pine Mountain Lake has plenty of residents who spent thirty years maintaining their own property and are no longer able to get up the ladder or spend a day with a rake. That is most of what this business is.

So the way the job is handled matters as much as the job itself:

  • Nobody is talked down to, and nobody is rushed into a decision.
  • No extras get added to a job without being asked about first.
  • Prices are written down in plain language before work starts.
  • Family members out of the area can be kept in the loop by phone, email or photos.
  • If a job needs a licensed trade instead, Bryan says so rather than taking the work.
