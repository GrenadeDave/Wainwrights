Bryan Wainwright

The short version

A neighbor with a truck, a trailer and a long list of skills

Wainwrights Handyman & Land Management is Bryan Wainwright — one person, working in and around Pine Mountain Lake. There is no call center, no dispatcher and no rotating crew. You call the number on this page and Bryan answers it.

The work splits into two halves. Inside and on the house, it is repairs and painting — the jobs that accumulate quietly until a whole weekend disappears into them. Outside, it is land management — clearing needles, brush and yard waste, opening defensible space back up, and hauling away whatever needs to go.

Both halves come down to the same thing: turning up when he said he would, doing the job properly, and leaving the place tidy.

How he works

Four things you can count on

He shows up

If Bryan says Tuesday morning, it is Tuesday morning. If something goes wrong, you get a call rather than silence.

Price before work

A written figure first, every time. You always know what you are agreeing to before anything begins.

Plain answers

No jargon and no pressure. Ask as many questions as you like — that is part of the job, not an interruption.

Left tidy

Debris loaded and hauled off, tools packed up, and a walk-through together before he leaves.

Working with older neighbors

A lot of this work is for people who used to do it themselves

Pine Mountain Lake has plenty of residents who spent thirty years maintaining their own property and are no longer able to get up the ladder or spend a day with a rake. That is most of what this business is.

So the way the job is handled matters as much as the job itself:

  • Nobody is talked down to, and nobody is rushed into a decision.
  • No extras get added to a job without being asked about first.
  • Prices are written down in plain language before work starts.
  • Family members out of the area can be kept in the loop by phone, email or photos.
  • If a job needs a licensed trade instead, Bryan says so rather than taking the work.

Bigger jobs, and anything needing a permit, go to a licensed contractor — he will say so and often knows who to call.
